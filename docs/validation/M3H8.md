# M3h8 — Doors, lifts, floors, switches and the exit (Stage B)

This record owns observed engineering results. The
[investigation](../M3H8_REFERENCE_INVESTIGATION.md) owns source facts, the admitted
domain, the proofs (§4–§6), the load rules (§7), the budgets (§8), the staging
measurement (§9) and the plan (§12); [format](../STATE_FORMAT.md#dsim-v35--original-doors-lifts-floors-switches-and-the-exit)
owns DSIM35 and [support](../COMPATIBILITY.md#original-monsters-with-doors-lifts-floors-switches-and-the-exit)
the admitted behavior. Exact review state belongs to GitHub. No full-player,
whole-ticker, DOS-demo or whole-game equivalence is claimed, and E1M1 itself is not
admitted.

**This record is incomplete.** It holds what was observed through plan step 15 (code at
`f4e38fe`), the independent review of plan step 18 with its fixes (`bfbc2b3`,
`a70e17c`, `66c5e18`, `92ec816`; the evidence counts below are those after the fixes)
and the matched timings of plan step 16 (first run at `1be8a29`; after the performance
fixes `b552b43`, `2ece00b` and `c6711f8`, run again at `c6711f8`).
The sections marked **PENDING** hold no result yet; they are filled by plan steps 17
and 19, and nothing in them may be read as a pass.

The observations below ran on the owner's local host: an Intel Core i7-8750H (12 logical
CPUs), 7 GiB RAM, Linux 6.18.58.1 (WSL2); rustc stable 1.99.1 and nightly 1.101.0
(2026-11-02); gcc 15.2.0; Python 3.14.4. The replay, test and fuzzing sections are
correctness observations, not performance measurements, and no timing in them is a
measurement. Measured timings are the staging comparison of the investigation's §9 and
[matched performance](#matched-performance), each with its own conditions.

## Original-C evidence

`tools/reference/m3h8_reference.py --check --overflow-check --address-check` (with
`ASAN_OPTIONS=detect_leaks=0`, so LeakSanitizer was disabled, not passed) compiles the
genuine door, plat, floor, switch, use, crossing and `P_ChangeSector` bodies listed in
`tests/reference/m3h8_contract.json` and runs 33 scenarios over the `doorway`, `hall`,
`switchyard`, `lift` (ceilings 192, 118 and 112), `floors`, `dense` and `pit` rooms. Three
scenarios were added by the review fixes: `door_chase_entry` and `lift_chase_entry` use
a new controlled between-tic input (`chase_at`: target assignment plus
`P_SetMobjState(mo, mo->info->seestate)`, whose `A_Chase` runs at once), and
`floor_turbo_below_500` runs a turbo floor whose only neighbour floor lies at −508
(destination −492). Regenerating the evidence left every earlier scenario's rows and
every one of the 13 fixtures unchanged; it appended rows, captures, event frames and the
maps `m3h8_geometry_19.bin`–`21.bin`. The strict
build and both sanitizer builds (`-fsanitize=signed-integer-overflow,integer-divide-by-zero,float-cast-overflow`
without `-fwrapv`, and `-fsanitize=address,undefined`, both `-fno-sanitize-recover=all`)
produced byte-identical raw traces; the contract records the digest of all three as
`sanitizer_comparison.raw_trace_sha256` and the expanded trace as
`expanded_trace_sha256`. `spechit` keeps its source size of eight in the harness; no
sanitizer report was produced. Differences from the investigation's §11 scenario list
(decision N1 option iii, combined scenarios, the Rust-only exit refusal, the evidenced
reversed `blazeOpen` door, V′ case (e) not constructed) are recorded in its §15 step 3.
After decision N9a the committed geometry names `SW1COMM` on every 11/63 front side and
the harness resolves textures through a declared table; the regenerated expanded trace
was byte-identical to the earlier one (§15).

## Rust source replay

`tools/reference/check_player_movers.py` runs the library test
`movers_replay::full::full_replay_matches_the_c_rows_and_the_independent_dsim35_goldens`
in debug and release and the `player_movers` example in both profiles. Observed after
the review fixes (at plan steps 13 and 14 it was 30 scenarios and 7,001 ops): all 33
scenarios match through their last op, 7,264 ops and event frames (83,667 event records,
equal to the golden stream), all 1,559 captures (digest, size, field count and the
`Diagnostic::decode_movers` round trip), the 13 fixtures byte for byte, 17,370
thinker-list rows with every mover record and 108 button rows, plus the partial
comparisons (48 crossings, 242 uses and intercepts, 202 blocked moves). The debug and
release summaries were equal, and so were the example's outputs. Before the fixes the
replay failed at `door_chase_entry` op 1 (sector 2 ceiling) and, with that fixed, at
`floor_turbo_below_500` op 23 (floor destination −500 instead of −492).
The example replays, through the public API alone, the 24 scenarios whose declared
inputs are public and asserts the C rows' headline counts. Two corrections the replay
found while it was written (the declared `script` 30 setup and the position of a
button's restore sound in the independent encoder) are recorded in §15 step 13.

**Every output field and every digest of all 222 workloads is byte-identical between
`37d4e5c` and `c6711f8`.** Across all 222 workloads, the median of the median deltas is
-5.2% and the median of the minimum deltas is -4.9%. Medians range from -19.4% to +8.7%,
and minimums from -18.0% to +5.8%. By example median, the player examples from
`player_pistol` to `player_specials` in the table are now 6–10% faster than master per tic
`advance`. `original_player`, `player_commands`, `player_knockback`, `player_demon` and
`player_demon_melee` are within -2.8% to +2.7%. The first run had every one of these 4–12%
slower. The `original`-profile stepping of `diagnostic_free` (`canonical_small`,
`actor_capacity`) moved from +16%/+18% to -6.8%/-5.2%. Map preparation, the historical profiles and the construction workloads stay
within about ±3% by example median.

**What remains above +3%.**

- `original_player`, +2.7% median of medians. Its `damage_death` and `demon_source`
  minimums are +4.7% and +4.9%. These are 60–90 µs timelines of one idle or barely
  active player, so a fixed per-tic cost shows most here. Callgrind on `idle` measures
  +1.9% instructions inside `Simulation::advance`, about 110 instructions on a tic of
  about 5,800. They come from the movers fields that every checked plan now copies in
  and back out (`StagedWrites`, the 128-byte `buttons`, `exit`), the staged sector view,
  and a few `Vec<Event>` extends the compiler no longer inlines. Boxing `buttons` out of
  the plan was tried and made no measurable difference, so it was reverted.
- `map_extent` `0 profile18` and `0`: +4.0% and +3.9% on the median, but +0.9% and -0.2%
  on the minimum. Its `27` and `29` workloads read +2.5%/+3.0% and +3.6%/+3.5%. These
  1.2–1.7 ms map preparations read +2% median in the first run as well. Callgrind
  measures +2.0% to +2.7% instructions in their (small) `advance`, from the movement and

### Investigation of the first run's slowdown

`valgrind` 3.26.0 was extracted without installation from the Ubuntu package
(`apt-get download valgrind`, `dpkg -x` into `~/scratch/m3h8/vg`, `VALGRIND_LIB` set). Its
callgrind counted instructions inside `Simulation::advance` only
(`--toggle-collect='<doom_sim::Simulation>::advance'`). The release examples of both
trees were rebuilt with `CARGO_PROFILE_RELEASE_DEBUG=line-tables-only`, which leaves code
generation unchanged. Instruction counts are deterministic, so a fix's effect could be
read without timing noise. Timings confirmed each step. These callgrind runs and the
builds were not timing measurements, and no timing ran during them.

**At `d1a047d` (the first run's code), instructions per timeline inside `advance`:**
`player_pistol first_shot` +8.7% (2,259,877 → 2,455,499), `player_monsters pursuit` +8.6%
(26,757,754 → 29,055,154), and `diagnostic_free canonical_small original` +10.6%
(2,674,111 → 2,956,928). This is close to the measured timing deltas, so the slowdown was
mostly executed instructions, not cache or layout effects alone. Built with
`CARGO_PROFILE_RELEASE_CODEGEN_UNITS=1` (both trees, experiment only), the same deltas
were +4.0%, +4.2% and +5.2%. About half of the excess therefore came from how the new
modules changed the release build's 16-unit code partition and its inlining.

**The four hypotheses of the task, checked:**

1. *A larger `Event`.* Refuted. `size_of::<Event>()` is 152 bytes on both trees
   (`MoverEvent` is 32), and `Actor` (136), the actor slot (208), `SectorThinker` (28)
   and the actor `ThinkerStore` (104) are unchanged as well.
2. *Larger hot structures.* These grew: `DemonPlan` from 496 to 688 bytes,
   `thinkers::Inputs` from 304 to 336 and `Simulation` from 1,384 to 1,672. The sector
   view `staging::Sectors` is two slices (32 bytes) where master passed one (16).
   In callgrind, `demon_plan`'s own copy costs the same 30 instructions per tic on both
   trees. Boxing `buttons` out of the plan changed nothing measurable and was not
   kept.
3. *Work only movers need.* Every new movers check was already behind a cheap flag
   (`spechit` returns at once without line specials, crossings are only collected for a
   non-empty `spechit`, `gibbed` tests `has_movers()` first). The cost was in how the
   checks compiled. `monster_actor` gained a 136-byte copy of the validated actor per
   call (+40 instructions; 18,392 calls in `pursuit`), because `self.gibbed(&a)` needed
   the actor in memory. `perception_actor`'s bound check (`position().into_iter().chain([z])`)
   and `require_role_for` stopped being inlined, which cost a further +1.1M and +0.4M
   instructions in `pursuit`. The thinker pass built the staged view twice per visit,
   and `move_xy_profile` built its larger `Mover` before testing for zero momentum.
4. *The `original`-profile stepping (+16–18%).* It runs the same checked-plan path as
   the player examples (`advance_expanded_demons`, `preflight_monster_owner` and
   `monster_actor` per monster per tic). It is not a snapshot or encoding path, and the
   same causes explain it.

A further cost that predates the branch showed up in the weapon examples. `weapons.rs`
looked up `S_PLAY`, `S_PLAY_ATK1`/`2`, `S_PLAY_RUN1`, `S_CHAIN1` and every weapon's five
states by a linear string search of the source state table on every psprite step.
Master's build inlined these searches into `Machine::enter`. The branch's build did
not, so `memcmp` calls doubled.
